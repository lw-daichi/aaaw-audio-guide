アイヌの人々は自然や動植物のみならず日々の暮らしに用いる道具や日用品にもカムイ（魂）が宿ると考えてきた。その役目を終えた道具は神の世界へと送られる。今回阿寒の地で出会い拾い集めた、かつて誰かの生活や仕事を支えていたものたちをあらためて組み合わせ、ひとつの場に配置する。役目を終えたものに新たな関係を与え・手を動かし・ものと場の組み合わせを考える。そんな行為のなかにあるつくることの悦びそのものを、ひとつの供物としてこの地に捧げたい。

===

The Ainu have held that kamuy (spirit) dwells not only in nature and in living things but also in the tools and everyday objects used in daily life. A tool that has finished its role is sent off to the world of the gods. Here I take the things I encountered and gathered in Akan—things that once supported someone's life or work—and combine them anew, arranging them in a single place. To give new relationships to things whose roles have ended, to move my hands, to think through the combinations of objects and place: the very joy of making that lies within such acts, I wish to offer to this land as a kind of oblation.
