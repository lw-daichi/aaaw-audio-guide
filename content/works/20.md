アイヌの人々にとって、木を刻み出す行為は日常と祈りのあわいにありました。彫り出された造形は、その日のうちに燃やすという習わしがあり、形は永遠に残されるものではなく、祈りとともに天へ還されていきました。

イクパスイは神に祈りを届ける神具であると同時に、この上でだけ偶像を刻むことが許された特別な場であり、後には偶像を独立したパーツとして取り付けることも可能となりました。
本作は、信仰と創造、消失と持続のあわいに人間が行ってきた「形をつくる営み」と自らの祈りや想像を重ね合わせます。

===

For the Ainu people, the act of carving wood lay in the space between daily life and prayer. There was a custom of burning the carved forms within the same day; the forms were not meant to remain forever but were returned to the heavens together with the prayer. The ikupasuy is a sacred implement for delivering prayers to the gods, and at the same time a special place—the only place upon which the carving of figures was permitted; in later times it also became possible to attach figures as separate, independent parts. This work overlays the artist's own prayer and imagination onto the human "work of making forms" carried out in the space between faith and creation, between disappearance and endurance.
