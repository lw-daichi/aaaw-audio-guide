・チカㇽカㇽペ
アイヌ語で「私たちが刺繍したもの」を意味する木綿の衣服。紺や黒の布を切って衣服に縫い留め、その上から刺繍を重ねて文様を描き出します。縫い目もまた、文様の一部になります。文様は女性たちが幼い頃から練習を重ねて身につけてきたもので、地域やつくり手によって表情が異なります。一着の衣服には、文様を学び、一針ずつ縫い進めてきた時間が重なっています。

・チンジㇼ
木綿の布地に直接刺繍を施して文様を描き出した衣服です。布を重ねて縫い付けることはせず、糸の線だけで文様を表します。ここ阿寒ではチンジㇼと呼ばれています。一本一本の線には、つくり手の手の運びと、縫い進めてきた時間がそのまま残っています。

===

- ci-kar-kar-pe: A cotton garment whose name means, in the Ainu language, "the thing we have embroidered." Strips of indigo or black cloth are cut and sewn onto the garment, and embroidery is layered over them to draw out the patterns; the seams themselves also become part of the design. The patterns are ones women have acquired through practice since childhood, and their character differs by region and by maker. A single garment holds, layered within it, the time spent learning the patterns and sewing stitch by stitch.

- chinjiri: A garment whose patterns are drawn by embroidering directly onto the cotton fabric. No cloth is layered and sewn on; the patterns are expressed by the lines of thread alone. Here in Akan it is called chinjiri. Each single line preserves, just as it is, the movement of the maker's hand and the time spent sewing.
